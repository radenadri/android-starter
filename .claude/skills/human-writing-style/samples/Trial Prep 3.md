## **Part 1: Outline of Legal & Ethical Arguments**

### **A. Legal Arguments**

1. **No "Sale" Occurred and Ware Fray is Not a "Seller" under Virginia Law.**  
   * Ware Fray did not sell or contract to sell FreeMax; instead, he offered an open-source project where the Allmart scholarship was an independent award he applied for, which can not be deemed as payment for the software.  
   * **Supporting Law/Case:**   
     * The definition of a "seller" in the **Code of Virginia, § 8.2-103(1)(d)** requires a person who "sells or contracts to sell goods." Since no price was paid and no sale contract was formed, Fray does not meet this definition.  
     * § 8.2-103. Definitions and index of definitions., Va. Code Ann. § 8.2-103 (Current through 2025 Regular Session). [https://advance-lexis-com.mutex.gmu.edu/api/document?collection=statutes-legislation\&id=urn%3acontentItem%3a63TX-H321-DYB7-W0D2-00000-00\&context=1519360\&identityprofileid=QV2FFJ51580](https://advance-lexis-com.mutex.gmu.edu/api/document?collection=statutes-legislation&id=urn%3acontentItem%3a63TX-H321-DYB7-W0D2-00000-00&context=1519360&identityprofileid=QV2FFJ51580).

2. **No Implied Warranty of Fitness for a Particular Purpose Ever Existed.**  
   * There is no implied warranty because Fray articulated through multiple, conspicuous disclaimers that the software was untested and "as is"  and maintained a public list of known bugs.  
   * **Supporting Law/Case:**  
     * The Code of Virginia § 8.2-316(3)(a) is the controlling statute. It explicitly states that "*unless the circumstances indicate otherwise, all implied warranties are excluded by expressions like 'as is,' 'with all faults' or other language which in common understanding calls the buyer's attention to the exclusion of warranties and makes plain that there is no implied warranty*". Fray's use of an "as is" disclaimer in the source code falls directly under the protection of this statute.  
       * § 8.2-316. Exclusion or modification of warranties., Va. Code Ann. § 8.2-316 (Current through 2025 Regular Session). https://advance-lexis-com.mutex.gmu.edu/api/document?collection=statutes-legislation\&id=urn%3acontentItem%3a63TX-H321-DYB7-W0F6-00000-00\&context=1519360\&identityprofileid=QV2FFJ51580.  
     * In the case of King Industries, Inc. v. Worlco Data Systems, the court found that a disclaimer written in capital letters above the signature line was "conspicuous" enough to hold up legally. What's particularly relevant here is how the court placed the responsibility on the buyer. The judge stated that "In the instant case, the plaintiff King Industries as a prudent buyer was charged with the duty of conducting an investigation by reading the two contracts at issue." Given that, Allmart as the “consumer” had the same duty to investigate the software before deploying it as Fray gave them multiple warnings.  
       * King Industries, Inc. v. Worlco Data Systems, Inc., 736 F. Supp. 114, 1989 U.S. Dist. LEXIS 17041, 11 U.C.C. Rep. Serv. 2d (Callaghan) 1146 (United States District Court for the Eastern District of Virginia, Norfolk Division March 3, 1989 ). [https://advance-lexis-com.mutex.gmu.edu/api/document?collection=cases\&id=urn%3acontentItem%3a3S4N-CV90-0054-42CW-00000-00\&context=1519360\&identityprofileid=QV2FFJ51580](https://advance-lexis-com.mutex.gmu.edu/api/document?collection=cases&id=urn%3acontentItem%3a3S4N-CV90-0054-42CW-00000-00&context=1519360&identityprofileid=QV2FFJ51580).  
     * Additionally, in the case of Parker-Smith v. STO Corp, the court found that even when a product failure led to destruction, a valid disclaimer is still enforced. The judge ruled that "even if the limited warranty fails in its essential purpose, an exclusion of consequential damages is enforceable if not unconscionable." What makes a disclaimer unconscionable is "grossly unequal bargaining power" between the parties. Given that the relationship here was between a large corporation and a student offering free software, there was no such imbalance that would render Fray's disclaimer unconscionable. Therefore, any reliance Allmart placed on Fray was unreasonable, and no implied warranty was ever created.  
       * Parker-Smith v. Sto Corp., 52 Va. Cir. 577, 1999 Va. Cir. LEXIS 709, 43 U.C.C. Rep. Serv. 2d (Callaghan) 606 (Circuit Court of Fairfax County, Virginia December 3, 1999, Decided ). [https://advance-lexis-com.mutex.gmu.edu/api/document?collection=cases\&id=urn%3acontentItem%3a42H2-BJ20-0039-43C9-00000-00\&context=1519360\&identityprofileid=QV2FFJ51580](https://advance-lexis-com.mutex.gmu.edu/api/document?collection=cases&id=urn%3acontentItem%3a42H2-BJ20-0039-43C9-00000-00&context=1519360&identityprofileid=QV2FFJ51580).

       

3. **Even If a Warranty Existed, It Was Not Breached.**  
   * Assuming, for the sake of argument, that an implied warranty was created, the plaintiff cannot prove it was breached. The circumstances in this case do not meet the threshold for a breach of warranty established by Virginia courts in cases involving software systems. The software was not "unfit"; rather, Allmart's negligence in the implementation of beta software was the cause of its damages.  
   * **Supporting Case:**   
     * To prove a breach, Allmart would need to show that FreeMax fundamentally failed to do what it was supposed to do. The bar for that is high, as shown in a precedent case, Micro Prods. v. Sylvan Learning Sys, where the court found a breach because the video-conferencing system worked less than half the time as stated; "successful connection rate... of less than fifty percent" and had persistent freezing and audio issues. The court ruled that Micro delivered a "system which did not function as represented."  
     * Here, Allmart can't make a similar claim as a single bug that causes a temporary crash doesn't rise to the level of "near-total failure" that the Micro Prods court dealt with. What's more telling is that Savana.com used the exact same software successfully in production. Rudy Kipling's testimony made that clear that his company used FreeMax successfully in their production environment "without any problems... because we conduct extensive testing."  
     * What's critical here is that the court in Micro Prods. found a breach because the seller failed to deliver a system that met the buyer's needs after the buyer "relied upon the expertise of Micro" as a professional "Value Added Reseller." Ware Fray isn't a professional reseller; he's just a student who made no representations of expertise and explicitly warned Allmart that the software was untested. Furthermore, he did not supervise the implementation of the software, giving some form of implicit warranty, unlike in the Micro Prods case. Therefore, to reiterate, the true cause of Allmart's damages wasn't a minor, fixable bug, but its own negligence in deploying experimental software into a mission critical system without the proper testing and safeguards that are standard industry practice.  
       * Micro Prods. v. Sylvan Learning Sys., 49 Va. Cir. 24, 1999 Va. Cir. LEXIS 265 (Circuit Court of Fairfax County, Virginia March 16, 1999, Decided ). https://advance-lexis-com.mutex.gmu.edu/api/document?collection=cases\&id=urn%3acontentItem%3a3YN2-KRW0-0039-4481-00000-00\&context=1519360\&identityprofileid=QV2FFJ51580.  
4. **The Proximate Cause of Allmart's Damages Was Its Own Negligence.**  
   * Allmart's financial losses weren't caused by the bug; they were caused by their own negligence, where their decision broke the chain of causation.  
   * **Supporting Law/Case:**   
     * Virginia's warranty law is made to protect consumers from physical harm caused by defective products they couldn't reasonably inspect or test. However, that protection doesn't extend to sophisticated commercial entities making poor business decisions.

### **B. Ethical Arguments**

1. **Ware Fray Acted Ethically by Fulfilling His Duties of Honesty and Transparency.**  
   * Fray was consistent about disclosing the state of FreeMax and set clear expectations about his limited availability for support.  
   * Fray's subsequent agreement with Savana was a separate business transaction that occurred after Allmart's damages were already caused by their own negligence. Fray had no existing duty to provide Allmart with a fix, and he accepted a paid offer to protect Savana's intellectual property that their engineers developed. This was not a malicious act against Allmart but an independent contract.  
   * **Deontology.** Fray fulfilled his moral duty to be truthful. In contrast, Allmart failed in its ethical duty to perform due diligence to protect its own systems and shareholders.  
2. **Ware Fray's Actions Generated More Overall Good Than Harm.**  
   * Fray created a valuable tool and offered it to the community for free, with positive intentions. This tool was successful in providing competitive advantages to responsible users like Savana.com.   
   * **Rule Utilitarianism.** The rule of creating and sharing open-source software produces the most good for the most people. Punishing a student for one company's negligence would create a net negative by discouraging future innovation and community contributions.

## **Part 2: Cross-Examination & Objections**

### **A. Cross-Examination Questions**

**Witness:** 

* **Sutton Prophet, CEO of Allmart**  
  * The scholarship awarded to Mr. Fray was based on his application, not on a contract to provide software, correct? (Get them to admit the scholarship was independent)  
  * Allmart awards scholarships to students who do not provide any products or services, isn't that right? (Get them to admit the scholarship was independent)  
  * There was no written agreement requiring Mr. Fray to deliver FreeMax to Allmart, was there? (No sale occurred, Element 1\)  
  * Allmart is a multi-billion dollar corporation with extensive resources, correct? (Undermine credibility: show bias, sophistication)  
  * You have a financial interest in the outcome of this case, don't you? (Show bias, financial interest in winning the case)  
  * Isn't it true that Allmart's IT department is responsible for vetting and testing all new software before deployment? (Get them to admit they didn't test properly)  
  * You were aware that Mr. Fray was a student and not a professional software vendor, correct? (No reasonable reliance Element 2c)  
  * The damages Allmart claims are purely economic, not related to any physical injury, correct? (Weaken their legal elements: not a consumer goods case)  
  * You were informed, prior to deployment, that FreeMax was untested and provided "as is," weren't you? (Get them to admit Fray gave warnings)  
  * Isn't it true that Allmart could have avoided these losses by conducting proper internal testing? (Their negligence caused damages Element 4\)  
  * You did not negotiate or pay a purchase price for FreeMax, did you? (No sale occurred Element 1))  
  * The decision to deploy FreeMax was made despite explicit warnings from Mr. Fray, correct? (Their negligence caused damages Element 4\)  
  * You have an obligation to shareholders to ensure due diligence in technology decisions, don't you? (Undermine credibility: show they failed their own standards)  
  * Isn't it true that Allmart's losses were the result of its own internal processes, not the software itself? (Their negligence caused damages (Element 4))  
  * You are aware that other companies used FreeMax successfully, correct? (Software wasn't unfit, they just didn't test (Element 3))  
  * Isn't it standard practice in your industry to thoroughly test open-source software before using it in mission-critical systems? (Get them to admit industry standard is user testing for open-source)  
  * Do you know how much time was spent internally testing the software? (Further reinforce the idea that it was due to their own negligence.)   
    1. Draw a comparison with Savana, regardless of the amount of time spent.   
       1. If they spent more time, how could they not find the bug that Savana found in less time?   
       2. If they spent less time, what didn’t they spend more time on testing, like how Savana did?  
* **Nero Valentine, Software Engineer**  
  * Isn't it true that Allmart's losses were the result of its own internal processes, not the software itself? (Their negligence caused damages (Element 4))  
  * "As a software professional, you'd agree that all software, especially beta software, can contain bugs, correct?" (Establishes that a bug is not unusual).  
  * "And because of that risk, isn't it the industry's standard of care to perform extensive testing on any third-party software before deploying it in a live, profit-generating environment?" (Establishes Allmart's duty).  
  * "You were hired after the system crashed, correct? So you have no personal knowledge of the testing procedures Allmart followed before they went live, do you?" (Limits his testimony to the aftermath, not the crucial pre-deployment phase).  
  * "Is it fair to say that a proper testing protocol is designed precisely to catch bugs like the one you found before they can cause financial damage?" (Connects the failure to test directly to the financial loss).

### **B. Evidentiary Objections**

**Opposing Witnesses:** 

* **Sutton Prophet**  
  * **Objection 1: Hearsay (Rule 5\)**  
    * Prophet is asked to state the specific amount of financial loss and its cause.  
    * **Why:** Prophet is repeating an out-of-court statement from his CFO to prove the truth (i.e., the cause and amount of the loss). Prophet lacks personal knowledge of the financial analysis; the CFO is the proper witness.  
  * **Objection 2: Lack of Personal Knowledge / Speculation (Rule 3\)**  
    * Prophet is asked to describe the technical failure.  
    * **Why:** As a CEO, Prophet did not personally inspect the code or the server logs. He can testify that sales were suspended, but he cannot testify as to why from a technical perspective. That is the role of an expert like Nero Valentine.  
  * **Objection 3: Improper Lay Witness Opinion**  
    * Prophet testifies with something along the line of "Given our communications, it was clear that Mr. Fray understood he was providing us with a commercial-grade product."  
    * **Why:** Prophet, a lay witness in this context, is offering a conclusion about "commercial-grade" software. He can testify about what was said in the emails, but he cannot give his opinion on their legal interpretation.  
  * **Objection 4: Relevance:**  
    * When the witness tries to discuss the Savana.com deal to make Fray look greedy  
    * **Why**: The Savana deal occurred after Allmart's deployment decision and after the bug occurred. It has no bearing on whether a sale occurred, whether a warranty existed, or what caused Allmart's damages.  
* **Nero Valentine**  
  * **Scenario 1: Offering a Legal Conclusion**  
    * After explaining the bug's technical function, ask a question that requires a legal judgment.  
    * **Why**: Valentine is an expert in software engineering, not law. He can testify that the bug caused pricing errors, but whether this constitutes a breach of an implied warranty is the ultimate question for the jury, not the witness.  
  * **Scenario 2: Opinion on Fray's Professional Conduct**  
    * He is asked, "Did the way Mr. Fray wrote this code meet the professional standard for commercial software?"  
    * **Why**: The question assumes that FreeMax was a commercial product, which is a point of dispute. Fray created it as a non-commercial, open-source project.
