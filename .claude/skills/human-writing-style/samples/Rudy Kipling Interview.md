A. Ware Fray (Defendant, Creator of FreeMax)

1\. Defendant was a “seller” of goods

* Mr. Fray, what is FreeMax and why did you create it?   
  * It’s an open-source software project I started as a fun learning exercise and to use as a portfolio piece to help me get a job. It was always marked with an “as is” disclaimer and an open-source license, though the license was mistakenly missing from the website for about a week.  
* Did you ever sell FreeMax to anyone or charge money for it?   
  * No, never. I never expected to make any profit from it.  
* Did you enter into a formal contract with Allmart for their use of FreeMax?   
  * No. Their CEO emailed me, and I gave them permission to use it. I told them it was beta software. They mentioned it would help my application for a scholarship I had already applied for separately.  
* Was the “Allmart Scholarship” a payment for the software?   
  * No. It was a scholarship I applied for on my own, and it was never tied to Allmart’s use of FreeMax in any agreement.

2\. An implied warranty of fitness for a particular purpose existed

* What did you tell Allmart about the software’s condition?   
  * I told them it was beta software, that it was open-source, and that it was provided “as is.” I also made sure that all known bugs were posted on my website for transparency and told them to contact me first if an issue came up.  
* Did you promise to provide ongoing support or guarantee it would work for Allmart’s specific purpose?   
  * No. I made it clear I didn’t guarantee its use for any specific purpose. There was no promise of hands-on support.  
* Were you involved in Allmart’s process of testing or integrating the software?   
  * No, I was not involved at all.  
* Were you aware that Allmart, a global retailer, intended to rely on your student project for its live, real-time pricing system?   
  * No. My understanding was that we would have casual contact. I had no expectation that they would use it for such a critical function without doing their own extensive work on it first.

3\. The implied warranty of fitness was breached

* Whose responsibility was it to test FreeMax before Allmart used it on their live website?   
  * It was Allmart’s responsibility. I assumed a user would be able to fix any bugs they encountered, which is common with open-source projects.  
* Did you warn Allmart about potential bugs?   
  * Yes, explicitly. I told them it was beta software and directed them to the list of known bugs on the website.  
* How did you first learn about the specific bug affecting Allmart?   
  * Interestingly, Rudy Kipling from Savana.com contacted me about a similar bug and shared their solution before Allmart ever reported the issue to me. Allmart’s CEO contacted me later to say they had a bug causing financial loss.  
* Can you describe your interactions with Savana.com compared to Allmart?   
  * I had much more contact with Savana. They reached out early and had technical questions every few days, which I answered. Rudy and I spoke almost weekly. They were very engaged in the process, which is very different from my interaction with Allmart.

4\. The breach caused the plaintiff to suffer damages

* Did you inform Allmart about your availability to fix potential bugs?   
  * Yes, I was clear that I had a busy schedule as a student and might not have time for maintenance or bug fixes.  
* Did Allmart offer to pay you or enter into a support contract before they launched the software?   
  * No. They only offered to pay me to fix the bug weeks after they had already started using the software and were losing money. They offered me the equivalent of a Starbucks salary.  
* Why didn’t you immediately fix the bug when they reported it?   
  * I was too busy with my schoolwork. I had already told them my time was limited.  
* Did Savana.com also have an arrangement with you regarding this bug?   
  * Yes. After Allmart experienced the bug, Rudy at Savana reached out. Eventually, I was offered about $10,000 a month from them, for a total of $40,000 so far, related to their solution.  
* In your view, were Allmart’s problems caused only by FreeMax, or might other factors have played a role?   
  * It’s possible other factors played a role. I’m not familiar enough with their systems to say for sure.  
* Let's discuss Savana.com. Did their CTO make you an offer after he learned Allmart was having problems?   
  * Yes, he did.  
* What exactly was that offer?   
  * He offered to pay me to keep Savana's bug fix a secret and not share it with their competitor, Allmart. He also offered me a job after I graduate.  
* Was there a formal contract for this payment? No, there was no contract.  
* And did you accept this arrangement? Yes, I accepted the offer.

B. Rudy Kipling (CTO of [Savana.com](http://Savana.com))

1\. Defendant was a “seller” of goods

(Note: Mr. Kipling's testimony does not directly address whether Ware Fray qualifies as a "seller.")

2\. An implied warranty of fitness for a particular purpose existed

* In your experience, what’s the usual expectation when using open-source software?   
  * The expectation is that the user assumes the risk. That’s why the software often includes disclaimers like the “as is” clause in the FreeMax source code. The user is responsible for their own testing and implementation.  
* Do companies normally rely on the student developer to maintain it?   
  * No, not at all. Our entire approach was based on the assumption that if anything went wrong, "we'd be able to fix it on our own."  
* Did Savana rely on Ware Fray personally, or did you handle the software internally?   
  * We handled it entirely internally. We have robust development and testing processes, and we never expected him to provide support.

3\. The implied warranty of fitness was breached

* Can you describe Savana’s use of FreeMax?   
  * We use it to customize our product pricing, which gives us a significant competitive advantage. We found it online and integrated it into our systems.  
* Did FreeMax generally work as intended for Savana?  
  *  Yes, absolutely. We have not had any problems with FreeMax in our production environment because we conduct extensive testing.  
* Were you aware of any bugs in FreeMax?   
  * Yes, during our testing, our team discovered a glitch that could occasionally offer items for sale below cost.  
* How did your company handle that bug? 	  
  * Our engineers developed a fix that prevented any damaging effects. We saw this as a normal part of implementing open-source software.

4\. The breach caused the plaintiff to suffer damages

* Did Savana suffer the same losses Allmart claims? Why or why not?   
  * No, we did not. We avoided any losses because we are a technology company with extensive testing procedures. I believe the damages stem from Allmart’s internal processes, not the software itself.  
* From your experience, could Ware Fray have foreseen that a company would suffer major damages by using FreeMax?   
  * I don't believe so. He provided the software with disclaimers and couldn't have reasonably foreseen a company would use it without proper testing and safeguards.  
* Did you speak with Ware Fray after you learned Allmart was having problems with the software?   
  * Yes. He told me that Allmart had offered to pay him to fix the bug.  
* What was your reaction to that news?  
  * I didn't want our competitor to benefit from our hard work. We developed the fix, and I considered it our trade secret.  
* What did you offer Mr. Fray?   
  * I offered to pay him to keep our fix a secret and to not develop an independent fix for Allmart. I also told him we would love to hire him after graduation.  
* How much did you pay Mr. Fray to keep this information from Allmart?   
  * I have paid him $40,000 so far, at a rate of $10,000 a month.  
* So, to be clear, you paid a student developer $40,000 specifically so he would not help your competitor, Allmart?   
  * I paid him to keep our trade secret safe.
