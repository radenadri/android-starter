Should state money fund afterschool STEM C+ clubs?  
With rapid advancements in science and technology, the question is how to best prepare students for the STEM+C workforce. The proposed STEM+C Competition Team Grant Program in Virginia (SB5) aims to address this critical issue by fostering student engagement, creativity, and interest in STEM+C careers. Evidence strongly suggests that STEM+C Competition Team Grants and other extracurricular STEM+C programs have far-reaching benefits for students, including fostering engagement, creativity, and laying the groundwork for future STEM+C careers.

Participation in STEM+C competitions and clubs can directly boost student engagement and creativity. Hands-on, project-based competitions and activities allow students to delve into STEM+C concepts in ways that ignite their curiosity and excitement. For example, studies investigating the impact of project-based learning with robotics have demonstrated an increase in creativity and positive attitudes toward STEM education (Siwa Pinasa & Luksanawadee Srisook, 2019). Similarly, Balogun and Miller (2022) argue that STEM clubs, specifically a drone club, provide a valuable and engaging way to introduce secondary students (grades 6-12) to STEM-related career possibilities and skills. This kind of learning environment, removed from the pressures of traditional assessment, encourages exploration, problem-solving, and the development of innovative solutions. For some students, these experiences can fundamentally shape their relationship with STEM+C fields.

Additionally, participation in STEM+C competitions and extracurricular activities can lay the groundwork for future STEM+C career aspirations. Through these experiences, students are exposed to real-world applications of STEM+C concepts, often working alongside mentors and professionals in the field (Jiang et al., 2022). Community-engaged engineering programs, such as the STEM Challenge at Texas Tech University, provide opportunities for students to work in teams and apply skills like communication, collaboration, critical thinking, and creativity to real-world engineering design problems. These experiences broaden their understanding of potential career paths and make STEM+C professions seem more tangible and attainable.

Moreover, extracurricular STEM+C programs can have a significant impact on skill development. Merino-Armero, González-Calero, & Cózar-Gutiérrez (2023) investigate whether participation in after-school robotics classes has an impact on elementary students' computational thinking skills. Their findings suggest that extracurricular programs focusing on robotics can support the development of computational thinking in elementary education, highlighting the potential impact on student learning outcomes.

Furthermore, ensuring equitable access to STEM+C opportunities is a critical issue that must be addressed when considering the implementation of programs like the STEM+C Competition Team Grant Program. Socioeconomic background and gender are two significant factors that can influence a student's ability to participate in and benefit from STEM+C experiences.

Students from low-income families or underserved areas may face barriers to accessing STEM+C programs due to limited resources, such as a lack of funding for materials, transportation, or qualified instructors. The STEM+C Competition Team Grant Program aims to bridge this equity gap by providing direct funding and resources to schools in these areas, enabling them to offer high-quality STEM+C experiences to their students. Without targeted initiatives like this, students from disadvantaged backgrounds may miss out on the numerous benefits associated with STEM+C engagement, including increased creativity, skill development, and exposure to potential career paths.

Gender disparities in STEM+C fields are another pressing concern. Despite efforts to promote gender equality in STEM+C education, girls remain underrepresented in these fields. Vrieler et al. (2021) highlight the complexities of this issue, noting that while girls and boys may hold similar views of computer science itself, girls are less likely to aspire to careers in CS. This finding suggests that simply providing access to STEM+C programs may not be sufficient to address the gender gap in STEM+C fields. To effectively encourage girls' interest and participation in STEM+C, educators must tailor their approaches to account for gender-based differences in preferences, learning styles, and social influences.

The positive impact of these grant programs and extracurricular activities suggests they can be a significant step towards resolving skill gaps and promoting equity in the future STEM+C workforce. While the benefits are clear, there are potential challenges to consider with the STEM+C Competition Team Grant Program. For instance, some research suggests that while the competitive nature of the grants can ignite excitement and short-term engagement, it may not fully address the broader issue of fostering sustained interest and equitable access to STEM+C education for all students. Longitudinal studies tracking participants' career paths would give clearer insights into the effectiveness of these programs in influencing long-term STEM+C trajectories.

In conclusion, ensuring equitable access to STEM+C opportunities is a challenge that requires targeted initiatives and tailored approaches. Programs like the STEM+C Competition Team Grant Program play a crucial role in providing resources and support to underserved communities, helping to bridge the socioeconomic equity gap. However, addressing gender disparities in STEM+C fields requires a more nuanced approach that takes into account the unique barriers and influences that may affect girls' interest and participation in STEM+C. By prioritizing both socioeconomic and gender equity in the design and implementation of STEM+C programs, we can work towards a more inclusive and diverse STEM+C workforce.

Source 1:   
Balogun, A. O., & Miller, J. (2022). Drone Club: Exploring Engineering and Employability Skills Outside the Classroom. TechTrends, 66(6), 923–930. [https://doi.org/10.1007/s11528-022-00798-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11528-022-00798-7)  
Balogun and Miller (2022) argue that STEM clubs, specifically a  drone club, provide a valuable and engaging way to introduce secondary students (grades 6-12) to STEM-related career possibilities and skills. The authors describe a drone club curriculum designed for both student participants and potential instructors, focusing on hands-on activities to promote project-based learning. The authors' purpose is to provide educators and STEM-industry professionals with the tools to launch successful drone clubs, with the ultimate goal of increasing secondary students' interest and skill-building in STEM fields. This article offers a practical framework for implementing STEM clubs and highlights the potential impact on student engagement and interest in STEM careers.

Jiang, Zhang & Lv (2022) argue that participation in STEM competitions can positively influence students' future career choices and engagement in STEM fields. The authors gather data using a survey of STEM competition alumni and employ mixed methods to analyze their findings. The purpose of this study is to shed light on the long-term benefits of STEM competitions for career development and sustained interest in STEM disciplines. I chose this source because it provides evidence that STEM competitions could be a strong motivator for attracting and retaining students in STEM+C pathways.

Source 3:  
Merino-Armero, J. M., González-Calero, J. A., & Cózar-Gutiérrez, R. (2021). The effect of after-school extracurricular robotic classes on elementary students’ computational thinking. Interactive Learning Environments, 1–12. [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2021.1946564](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2021.1946564)  
Merino-Armero, González-Calero, & Cózar-Gutiérrez (2023) investigate whether participation in after-school robotics classes has an impact on elementary students’ computational thinking (CT) skills. Their study analyzes the relationship between participation in such classes (and duration thereof) with students' CT, while controlling for fluid intelligence. The authors seek to provide empirical evidence that extracurricular programs focusing on robotics can support the development of computational thinking in elementary education. This study offers insights into the value of extracurricular activities for boosting key STEM skills and highlights the potential impact on student learning outcomes.

Siwa Pinasa and Luksanawadee Srisook (2019) demonstrate that a STEM education project-based learning curriculum that incorporates robotics can enhance creativity and student attitudes toward STEM education. The authors conducted a study with grade 11 students in Thailand and evaluated its impact on students' creativity and attitudes. The purpose of this study is to investigate the effectiveness of project-based learning with robotics and its relationship to student creativity. I chose this source because it demonstrates that robotics can be a valuable tool in enhancing specific aspects of creativity within a STEM+C context.

Source 5:  
Vrieler, T., Nylén, A., & Cajander, Å. (2020). Computer science club for girls and boys – a survey study on gender differences. Computer Science Education, 1–31. [https://doi.org/10.1080/08993408.2020.1832412](https://doi.org/10.1080/08993408.2020.1832412)  
Vrieler et al. (2021) conducted a survey study to explore gender differences in views of computer science (CS) among students in a computer science club. Their findings reveal that girls and boys hold similar views of CS itself, but girls are less likely to aspire to careers in CS. The study also identifies a difference in how boys and girls discuss CS: girls tend to talk with family members, while boys talk to a wider range of people. These findings suggest that educators need to tailor their approaches to CS education to account for these gender-based differences in order to effectively encourage girls’ interest in CS.
