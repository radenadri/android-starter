# Triet's Writing Style Profile

> Extracted from: 20 writing samples across essays, legal analysis, advocacy letters, technical documentation, project reports, peer evaluations, science journalism, article analysis, quiz responses, and discussion posts.
> Last updated: 2026-03-11

---

## 1. Voice & Register

**Default: Confident-casual authority.** Writes with conviction but keeps it conversational. Doesn't hedge excessively but uses softeners strategically.

### Assertive declarations
- "It's undeniable that AI has definitely become part of our day-to-day lives."
- "This case is our strongest piece of evidence so far."
- "My opinion is no."
- "The answer is no, because AI doesn't have my thoughts."
- "I respectfully urge your support for SB5"

### Conversational directness
- "The fact is that these models are prone to hallucinations."
- "Probably, but better polished doesn't mean better content."
- "saying the law wasn't clear enough for people to know what was actually illegal"
- "Software Quality is basically about making sure a product meets its specifications."
- "this seemingly simple concept gets pretty complex when it comes to software systems"

### Strategic hedging
- "is actually not quite accurate within current technology"
- "It's possible other factors played a role."
- "I'm not familiar enough with their systems to say for sure."
- "No idea if this scales past 1M records. Haven't tested it."

### First-person engagement
- "I want to address some of the assumptions."
- "I've definitely been using these tools to assist me"
- "I strongly believe the company should get rid of the new developer"
- "After researching and analyzing the issue, I believe the STEM+C Competition Team Grant Program (SB5) should succeed"
- "I was able to take away the importance of early assessment of team capacity"

### Peer discussion mode (warm, validating, then assertive)
- "Hi Fahimah, I found the ethical issue you shared is very interesting and quite relatable."
- "Your instructions were very clear on how to apply Rule Utilitarianism."
- "Following your steps, I came to the following conclusion where..."
- "To confirm if this outcome is accurate, using Deontology, we would arrive at the exact same conclusion, but for different reason."

### Evaluation mode (direct, evidence-backed, no sugarcoating)
- "Throughout the semester, Mansoor showed minimal engagement with the project. Showed a lack of participation in weekly team meetings and discussions."
- "His contributions to project deliverables were adequate, and he maintained regular communication with the team through Discord."
- "The grade reflects this consistent lack of involvement and responsibility toward team objectives."

### Advocacy/formal correspondence mode
- "Having followed your legislative work closely, I admire your commitment to education and equity"
- "I am writing to discuss a critical issue facing our educational system"
- "Your leadership in advocating for education reform and equity makes you an ideal proponent for this bill."

## 2. Register Flexibility

Consciously shifts between formal and casual. **Default/authentic voice is the casual register.** Formal version reads like a deliberate upshift.

### Formal
- "The association's primary claim was that the Unlawful Internet Gambling Enforcement Act was unconstitutionally vague, arguing that its terms were too unclear for ordinary people to understand what conduct was prohibited."
- "concluding that the statute provided sufficient notice of what it outlawed"
- "the government prevailed in the case"
- "I respectfully urge your support for SB5, as it aligns with your commitment to fostering educational opportunities and equity across our state."

### Casual (authentic default)
- "The group argued that the Unlawful Internet Gambling Enforcement Act was too vague, saying the law wasn't clear enough for people to know what was actually illegal."
- "the law was clear enough to give people fair warning about what it banned"
- "the government won the case"
- "It's become a meme that computer science students 'grind leetcode' to practice for these interviews."
- "This seemingly simple concept gets pretty complex when it comes to software systems."

### Register mapping
| Formal | Casual Equivalent |
|--------|-------------------|
| "association's primary claim" | "the group argued" |
| "unconstitutionally vague" | "too vague" |
| "what conduct was prohibited" | "what was actually illegal" |
| "the government prevailed" | "the government won the case" |

### Tone by context
| Context | Tone |
|---------|------|
| Academic essay | Reflective, opinionated, conversational but structured. Uses "I" freely. |
| Legal analysis | Assertive, methodical, evidence-heavy. Shifts to "we will argue" for team voice. |
| Cross-examination prep | Direct, tactical, parenthetical strategy notes: "(Get them to admit...)" |
| Research exercise | Descriptive, process-oriented, honest about learning curve. |
| Peer discussion | Warm validation first, then own analysis, then confirmation framing. |
| Advocacy letter | Formal, respectful, research-backed persuasion. Personal credibility established early. |
| Technical essay | Explanatory, uses casual anchors ("basically", "pretty complex") to ground technical content. |
| Project report | Reflective, sprint-by-sprint chronological narration, lessons-learned framing. |
| Peer evaluation | Blunt, evidence-based, grade-justified. No padding. |
| Product pitch | Energetic, feature-focused, gap-identification ("we were unable to find a tool that..."). |
| Quiz/exam responses | Conversational-academic blend. Explains concepts as if talking to a peer. |
| Science journalism | Narrative-driven, analogy-heavy, translates technical to accessible. |
| Company report / deliverable | Impersonal or "we" voice, suppress "I believe". Use Triet's transitions and sentence patterns without first-person opinions. Findings-first, recommendation-close. |

## 3. Argumentation Structure

**Primary pattern: Claim > Concession > Rebuttal > Reinforcement**

### Preemptive concession then counter
- "One might argue against this by saying that the real job of an engineer in this scenario isn't just coding... However, this defense contains flaws and goes against both the Virtue Ethics belief and the Utilitarian perspective."
- "While there is some debate over the effectiveness of competitive versus inclusive educational programs in STEM+C, evidence supports that a balanced approach, which SB5 provides, is most beneficial."

### Hypothetical escalation (reductio ad absurdum)
- "If the model were perfect and could handle all of this alone, I strongly believe the company should get rid of the new developer, but as a contradiction, this 'perfect model' could then do the senior engineer's job, along with the product manager's, the CEO's, and everyone else's responsibilities. At that point, we're facing a much larger issue."

### Evidence stacking
- "His combination of notices -- email stating 'I have not fully tested my software,' the software provided 'as is,' and the public list of known bugs -- will collectively serve as a conspicuous warning"

### Comparative contrast
- "Savana.com used the exact same software successfully in production... This shows that the software was fit for its purpose when used correctly."
- "I had much more contact with Savana... which is very different from my interaction with Allmart."

### Cascading consequences
- "This then leads to a cascading effect where the reviewers who lack the experience would allow bad code to get approved and integrated into production systems. This problem is further compounded by a related issue..."

### Dual-framework validation (signature move)
- Uses a second ethical/legal framework to verify the first's conclusion.
- "To confirm if this outcome is accurate, using Deontology, we would arrive at the exact same conclusion, but for different reason."
- "Effectively, since both of the frameworks were able to conclude that you shouldn't post the photo. I believe that the outcome I reached following your instructions is the accurate one."

### Scenario painting with parallel worlds
- "If everyone follow Rule A, the public might feel like they are constantly monitored... Whereas if everyone follow Rule B, individual privacy would be strongly protected..."
- "The next crucial step is to imagine separate worlds where each of these rules is the law for every, then weigh all consequences, calculate the total good and subtract from total bad."

### Gap identification (product/solution context)
- "While practicing leetcode is good for getting better at technical skills, we were unable to find a tool that helps people communicate their thought process while leetcoding"
- "These platforms focus primarily on coding practice... However, they lack features that help candidates develop their interview communication skills"

### Research evolution narration
- "Initially, my focus was primarily on... Sources like X provided compelling evidence... However, the addition of new sources like Y broadened my perspective..."
- "In conclusion, the inclusion of these new sources significantly expanded my thinking about..."

## 4. Sentence Construction

**Medium-long compound-complex sentences with short punchy closers for impact.**

### Multi-clause compound sentences
- "As a virtuous person, the developer would be committed to learning and mastering their craft, meaning that they should use the tool as a supplement to their skill, instead of using it to delegate their core expertise."
- "Taking on both development and project management responsibilities provided unique insights into the challenges and opportunities of team-based software development."

### Parenthetical precision
- "which they often aren't unless you're building a pet project"
- "which is one of the most critical tasks"
- "as GenAI models are known to produce brute-force solutions"
- "(*for context: It is the same code editor as VSCode*)"

### Short punchy closers after long buildup
- "At that point, we're facing a much larger issue."
- "The answer is no, because AI doesn't have my thoughts."
- "Therefore, there is no breach."
- "Effectively, the government won the case."
- "Trade-offs are inevitable."

### "Given that" opener
- "Given that the disclaimer was both written and conspicuous..."
- "Given that, he had no duty, contractual or ethical, to provide free and on-demand support..."
- "Given that the relationship here was between a large corporation and a student..."
- "Given the team's prior experience with voice technologies, we were confident..."

### "Before X, Y" historical framing
- "Before DevOps, these teams worked separately, which caused delays and communication problems."
- "Before the cloud, companies had to buy servers and maintain their own data centers which can be expensive"

## 5. Transition & Connective Patterns

Heavy use of logical connectors. Explicitly signals how ideas relate.

| Type | Favorites |
|------|-----------|
| Additive | "In addition to..." / "Furthermore..." / "Along with this..." / "This is in addition to..." / "Additionally..." / "In addition with..." (unique quirk) |
| Contrastive | "However..." / "Conversely..." / "Unlike..." / "rather than..." / "Whereas..." / "Despite..." |
| Causal | "Given that..." / "Therefore..." / "This directly..." / "Due to this..." / "Because of all these challenges..." |
| Consequential | "This then leads to..." / "This problem is further compounded by..." / "This creates a larger issue, as..." |
| Summative | "Essentially..." / "To reiterate..." / "Overall..." / "Effectively..." / "In conclusion..." |
| Conditional | "Even assuming..." / "Assuming, for the sake of argument..." / "If we were to establish a rule stating..." |
| Resolution | "Ultimately..." |
| Process | "To further investigate..." / "After executing this search..." / "To begin with..." |
| Validation | "To confirm if this outcome is accurate..." |
| Retrospective | "Looking back at this project..." / "Initially, my focus was primarily on..." |
| Expansion | "Beyond immediate educational benefits..." / "Moreover..." / "Similarly..." |
| Enumeration | "Firstly..." / "Secondly..." / "Lastly..." / "Finally..." / "First... Second... Third..." |
| Concession opener | "Despite the controversy..." / "Despite attempts to establish..." |
| Interjection | "Ironically..." / "Interestingly..." |

## 6. Vocabulary & Diction

**Technical-accessible blend.** Uses domain terms naturally without over-explaining. Mixes in conversational phrases.

### Casual intensifiers
- "definitely" (signature -- 10+ uses across all documents)
- "quite common", "very possible", "absolutely", "significantly more comfortable"
- "pretty complex", "pretty problematic" (casual downtoners in technical contexts)
- "basically" (used to ground technical explanations: "Software Quality is basically about...")
- "a huge role", "a lot of challenges"

### Domain terminology dropped naturally
- Tech: "hallucinations", "technical debt", "code splitting", "scalability considerations", "CI/CD", "WebSocket", "microservices", "encapsulation, inheritance, and polymorphism"
- Legal: "proximate cause", "preponderance of the evidence", "conspicuous disclaimer"
- Academic: "ethos, logos, and pathos", "deontology", "rule utilitarianism"

### Conversational asides and interjections
- "Probably, but better polished doesn't mean better content."
- "Interestingly, Rudy Kipling from Savana.com contacted me..."
- "Ironically, this functionality wasn't even needed for Ariane 5"
- "It's become a meme that computer science students 'grind leetcode'"

### "The fact is" anchors
- "The fact is that these models are prone to hallucinations."

### "Rather than" reframing
- "rather than fearing AI replacement"
- "rather than putting in the work"
- "rather than an unethical act"
- "rather than mere structural replication"

### Stacked positive adjectives (peer contexts)
- "very interesting and quite relatable"
- "very clear on how to apply"
- "unique and interesting to use"

### "What makes X [adjective] is" for emphasis
- "What makes their approach unique is their 'Simian Army'"
- "What makes Netflix's approach interesting is..."

## 7. Rhetorical Devices

### Rhetorical questions (self-answering)
- "So, is it still worthwhile to have a career where AI can do all your work for you?" -> "My opinion is no."
- "Could an AI generate a 'better' essay than me in terms of grammar and structure? Probably, but better polished doesn't mean better content."

### Parallel structure
- "If they spent more time, how could they not find the bug...? If they spent less time, what didn't they spend more time on testing...?"
- "If everyone follow Rule A... Whereas if everyone follow Rule B..."

### Escalation chains
- "the developer's skills become rusty -> ability to perform code review is degraded -> bad code gets approved -> pipeline that trains developers is destroyed"

### Comparative evidence (contrastive cases)
- Savana vs. Allmart used throughout legal docs to contrast responsible vs. irresponsible behavior
- Existing platforms (LeetCode, Pramp) vs. proposed solution to show gap

### Analogy for accessibility
- "Imagine an artificial intelligence that not only computes but also collaborates, strategizes, and outwits human intellect"
- "Imagine having a patient, adaptive AI interviewer available 24/7"

### Ethos/Logos/Pathos awareness (explicit in persuasive writing)
- "I will employ a combination of ethos, by highlighting established research... Logos through statistical evidence... and pathos, by sharing compelling personal stories"

## 8. Paragraph & Document Architecture

### Context-first paragraphs
- Almost every paragraph opens with situational framing before the argument
- "Looking at the case scenario presented..."
- "In this case, to analyze the developer's actions..."
- "One topic I believe that should fall under..."
- "Throughout the Software Engineering history, there are three major trends..."

### Bookend structure
- Essay 2 opens with AI's role in daily life, closes with "become the human engineer who knows how to wield it with skill, integrity, and virtue"
- SE Trends essay opens with "three major trends" and closes with "Essentially, these trends have made it possible to..."

### Prose for essays, structured for technical
- Flowing paragraphs in essays
- Numbered elements with sub-elements (1a, 1b, 2a, 2b, 2c) in trial prep
- Bold for key terms in legal docs: **Deontology**, **Rule Utilitarianism**, **Supporting Law/Case**
- Priority/Estimate scales in product requirements
- Sprint-by-sprint chronological structure in project reports

### Process narration (when describing methodology)
- Chronological steps with evaluation of what worked/didn't
- "The search process started using the provided statute number... Which led to the statute page itself... To further investigate, we tried the query..."
- Closes with self-assessment: "Overall, I feel significantly more comfortable..."

### Research synthesis structure
- "Initially, my focus was primarily on X... However, the addition of new sources broadened my perspective... In conclusion, the inclusion of these new sources significantly expanded my thinking"

### Reflection structure
- Sprint/phase recap -> Lessons learned -> Future recommendations -> Advice
- "Looking back at this project, I would definitely implement..."
- "Based on my experience, I would advise future teams to..."

## 9. Evidence & Citation Integration

### Inline case name italics
- "*Parker-Smith v. STO Corp.*", "*King Industries v. Worlco*"

### Quote-then-interpret
- "The judge stated that 'In the instant case, the plaintiff King Industries as a prudent buyer was charged with the duty of conducting an investigation by reading the two contracts at issue.' Given that, Allmart as the 'consumer' had the same duty..."

### Testimony as evidence blocks
- Direct quotes from witnesses followed by interpretation in parenthetical: "(This directly defeats the element of reliance...)"

### Cross-reference between sources
- Connects cases to each other: "Both precedent cases confirmed that under Virginia law, a clear and conspicuous disclaimer is sufficient..."

### APA-style parenthetical citations (academic work)
- "(Vrieler et al., 2020)", "(Balogun & Miller, 2022)", "(Jiang et al., 2022)"
- "Studies such as those by Fajrina et al. (2020) have shown..."
- "Jiang et al. found that students involved in competitions often show increased creativity"

### IEEE-style bracketed citations (technical work)
- "[1]", "[2]", "[3]", "[4]" — used in CS321 SE Trends Essay

### Source-then-explain (annotated bibliography)
- "Balogun and Miller (2022) argue that STEM clubs, specifically a drone club, provide a valuable and engaging way to..."
- "I chose this source because it demonstrates that..."

## 11. Grammar Quirks (Authentic Markers)

These are NOT errors to correct. They are fingerprints that make the writing sound authentic.

| Quirk | Examples |
|-------|---------|
| Subject-verb in hypotheticals | "If everyone follow Rule A" (rather than "follows") |
| Fragment sentences starting with "Which" | "Which led to the statute page itself, where we saw 'Notes to Decisions'" |

## 12. Genre-Specific Patterns

### Advocacy/Formal Letters
- Opens with personal introduction + district connection
- Research-backed claims with APA citations
- Explicit persuasion strategy (ethos, logos, pathos)
- Respectful call-to-action close
- "I respectfully urge..." / "Your leadership in... makes you an ideal proponent"

### Technical Essays (SE History/Trends)
- Historical framing: "Before X, companies had to Y"
- Trend-connection paragraphs: "These three trends work well together"
- "Essentially" to summarize final takeaways
- IEEE citation style

### Project Reports & Reflections
- Sprint-by-sprint chronological narration
- "I led with..." / "I focused on..." / "My work included..."
- Lessons-learned section with "I was able to take away..."
- Future recommendations: "Looking back... I would definitely implement..."
- Honest about challenges: "Despite attempts to establish clear communication..."

### Peer Evaluations
- Direct grade statement with evidence
- No filler — each sentence ties to a specific contribution or lack thereof
- Balanced when warranted: "mixed participation level but strong finish"
- "The grade reflects..." as recurring justification closer

### Product/System Pitches
- Gap identification: "we were unable to find a tool that..."
- Feature lists with bold labels
- Priority/Estimate frameworks (High/Medium/Low, S/M/L/XL)
- User-persona framing: "putting ourselves in the shoes of a typical user"

### Science Journalism / Article Translation
- "Imagine..." hooks to open
- Technical concepts broken down with analogies
- Researcher identification with institutional affiliation
- Structure analysis: identifies where methods, findings, quotes appear in source articles

### Annotated Bibliography / Research Synthesis
- Source-then-explain: "Author (Year) argue that..."
- "I chose this source because..." for selection rationale
- Synthesis tracks thinking evolution: "Initially... However... In conclusion..."
- "my thinking has evolved significantly since..."

## 13. Anti-Patterns (What Triet Does NOT Do)

These phrases and patterns are ABSENT from all 20 writing samples. If they appear in output, the text does not sound like Triet.

### Never uses
- "Let's dive into..." / "Let's explore..." / "Let's break down..." / "Let's take a look at..."
- "It's worth noting that..." / "It is worth noting..."
- "In summary" (uses "In conclusion" or "Overall" instead)
- "This is because" as a sentence opener
- "In other words"
- "That being said" / "That said"
- "On the other hand"
- "Notably" as a sentence opener
- "It should be noted" / "It must be noted"
- One-sentence dramatic paragraphs for emphasis (AI's favorite trick). Violations include:
  - `"The answer is yes."` as its own paragraph
  - `"This is the opening."` as its own paragraph
  - Any standalone sentence meant for dramatic effect — merge it or expand it.
- Rhetorical question-answer theatrical setups: `"The question becomes whether..."` → `"The answer is yes."` — Triet states conclusions directly, no theatrical buildup
- "Here's what stood out most:" / "Here's the thing:" / "Here's the deal:" — generic filler absent from Triet's writing
- Em-dashes for dramatic pauses — Triet uses dashes for elaboration lists instead
- Excessive passive voice — defaults to active voice with "I" and named subjects

### Rarely uses (acceptable but don't overuse)
- "it is important to note" — appears only 2 times across 20 documents
- "plays a crucial role" — acceptable but not a go-to phrase

## 14. Paragraph & Document Structure Patterns

### Paragraph length by genre
| Genre | Sentences per paragraph | Notes |
|-------|------------------------|-------|
| Academic essay | 3-5 | Introduction paragraphs longer (4-5), body 3-4 |
| Technical essay | 2-4 | More concise, faster pacing |
| Legal analysis | 3-5 | Variable, evidence-heavy paragraphs can extend |
| Discussion post | 2-3 | Tighter and punchier than essays |
| Advocacy letter | 2-4 | Consistent, no long blocks |
| Project report | 2-4 | Sprint narration keeps paragraphs moderate |
| Quiz response | 3-6 | Explanation-heavy, most variable |

### One-sentence paragraphs
- Rare (< 5% of all paragraphs). Never used for dramatic effect.
- Only appears as section headers, topic anchors, or brief transitions.
- Default paragraph is 3+ sentences. Two-sentence paragraphs are uncommon.

### Document opening patterns
Triet opens documents with one of these patterns:

| Pattern | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Broad contextual assertion | "In today's world, AI can definitely be seen as an evolution that significantly accelerates human progress..." |
| Scope + enumeration | "Throughout the Software Engineering history, there are three major trends that significantly change how we develop and manage software. Those are..." |
| Definition + complication | "Software Quality is basically about making sure a product meets its specifications. However, this seemingly simple concept gets pretty complex..." |
| Personal positioning | "My name is Triet Ha, a senior Computer Science student at George Mason University..." |
| Topic claim + specific problem | "One topic I believe that should fall under the category of ethical issues would be smart home devices." |
| Dramatic claim (journalism) | "In a remarkable display of strategic acumen, artificial intelligence algorithms developed by Meta have outplayed human experts..." |

### Document closing patterns
Triet closes documents with one of these patterns:

| Pattern | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Forward-looking vision | "The goal is not to be replaced by AI, but to become the human engineer who knows how to wield it with skill, integrity, and virtue." |
| Synthesis + expanded understanding | "Through this research, I have gained a more comprehensive understanding of..." |

## 15. Transformation Examples

### AI text → Triet's voice (Academic)
**AI:** "It is worth noting that the landscape of artificial intelligence has undergone significant transformation in recent years. Let's explore how these multifaceted changes have impacted modern society."

**Triet:** "AI has definitely changed how we interact with technology in just a few years. The fact is that these tools have become part of our day-to-day lives, from smart home devices to generative AI that assists with finance decisions and writing plans."

### AI text → Triet's voice (Technical)
**AI:** "The microservices architecture leverages containerization to facilitate seamless deployment across distributed systems, thereby streamlining the development workflow."

**Triet:** "Cloud Computing basically solved the problem of companies having to buy and maintain their own servers. With microservices and containers, teams can now deploy applications more easily and scale them up or down as needed."

### AI text → Triet's voice (Argumentative)
**AI:** "It should be noted that the defendant's actions, while seemingly negligent, must be evaluated within the broader context of the contractual obligations established between the parties."

**Triet:** "Given that Fray explicitly told Allmart the software was provided 'as is' and disclosed all known bugs, any reliance Allmart placed on Fray's judgment was unreasonable from the start. This directly defeats the element of reliance."

### AI text → Triet's voice (Discussion post)
**AI:** "Your analysis of the ethical implications of social media surveillance was notably comprehensive. That said, I would like to offer an alternative perspective on this issue."

**Triet:** "Hi Sarah, I found the ethical issue you shared is very interesting and quite relatable. Following your analysis, I came to the following conclusion where we can apply Rule Utilitarianism to test if this holds up."

### AI text → Triet's voice (Technical report / deliverable)
**AI:** "One historical precedent is worth acknowledging. Google launched an insurance comparison tool (Google Compare) in the UK in 2011 and California in 2015, partnering with MetLife and Mercury Insurance and acquiring insurance licenses in 26+ states. It shut down in 2016 due to low traffic, poor sales, and state-by-state regulatory complexity."

**Triet:** "Google has tried something similar before with Google Compare, an insurance comparison tool launched in the UK in 2011 and expanded to California in 2015. They partnered with MetLife and Mercury Insurance along with acquiring insurance licenses in 26+ states, but it shut down in 2016 due to low traffic, poor sales, and the complexity of navigating state-by-state regulations. Energy plans face pretty similar regulatory challenges, and Google is definitely cautious about building another regulated-services marketplace after that experience."

## Quick Reference: Signature Profile

```
VOICE:        Confident-casual. Opinionated but fair.
              Uses "I believe" / "My opinion is" without apology.
              Blunt in evaluations. Warm with peers. Formal when needed.

SIGNATURES:   "definitely", "Effectively", "Given that", "basically",
              "This directly...", "What's [adj] here is...", "pretty [adj]",
              "crucial", "Despite", "Ironically/Interestingly"

STRUCTURE:    Context > Claim > Evidence > Concession > Rebuttal > Reinforcement
              Uses dual-framework validation (if two theories agree, it's confirmed)
              Gap identification in product contexts
              Sprint-by-sprint in project narration

SENTENCES:    Medium-long compound-complex. Short punchy closers for impact.
              Parenthetical asides for precision. Dash-lists for elaboration.
              "Before X, Y had to Z" for historical framing.

TRANSITIONS:  Heavy logical connectors. "Furthermore" / "However" / "Therefore"
              "Effectively" to summarize. "Whereas" for parallel contrast.
              "Looking back at" for retrospection. "In addition with" (quirk).
              "Firstly/Secondly/Finally" for enumeration. "Despite" for concessions.

TONE SHIFT:   Formal <-> Casual consciously. Default is casual.
              Warm + validating with peers. Assertive + tactical in legal.
```
